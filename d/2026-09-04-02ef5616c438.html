🎭 Venetianische Messe — Venetian Fair, Ludwigsburg

Ludwigsburg city centre · Fri 4 – Sun 6 Sept · Free to walk the streets

Ludwigsburg's baroque old town is taken over by hundreds of costumed performers in full Venetian carnival dress — masks, capes, stilt-walkers, street theatre and music through the pedestrian zone. It is genuinely spectacular and unusually good for older kids, who tend to be drawn in by the costumes rather than bored by them. This is the rarity of the season: the fair is biennial, so if you miss it this weekend the next one is 2028.

Insider tip: S4 or S5 from Stuttgart Hbf to Ludwigsburg, roughly 15 minutes, then a short walk — far easier than parking, which is grim on fair weekends. Go late afternoon: the costumes photograph best in low sun and the crowd is still manageable before evening.

🍷 Stuttgarter Weindorf — Wine Village

Marktplatz & Schillerplatz, downtown Stuttgart · Through Sat 5 Sept · Free entry, pay per glass

Around 125 regional vineyards fill the city centre with wooden arbour huts pouring more than 500 local wines, alongside proper Swabian food. Closing weekend. The character shifts sharply by time of day: afternoons are a relaxed outdoor food market where families eat Maultaschen at communal tables, evenings become a dense adult crowd.

Insider tip: Arrive before 17:00 if you are bringing children — you will get a table, the food lines are short, and you will be gone before it fills up. Any S-Bahn to Stuttgart Hbf, then five minutes on foot.

🚶 Free English Walking Tour of Stuttgart

Meets Königstraße 1a · Every Sat & Sun, 11:00 · Free (tip-based), ~2 hours

A two-hour guided walk through the city centre in English — look for the guide with the yellow umbrella. Good orientation for anyone recently arrived, and a reasonable way to give teenagers some context for the city they have just been moved to. Covers the Schlossplatz, the old town and the city's rebuilding after 1945.

Insider tip: Best single thing to do in your first month at Panzer or Patch. No booking needed, but bring cash — the guides work for tips and card is rarely an option.

💡 Schlosslichtspiele — Palace Light Festival, Karlsruhe

Karlsruhe Palace · Through Sun 13 Sept · Free

Large-scale projection mapping across the full 170-metre facade of Karlsruhe Palace, this year under the motto "Lightyard Stories: Visions of Urban Living Spaces." Multiple artist-designed sequences run in rotation each night on a loop, so you can arrive whenever and still see complete pieces. Genuinely impressive at scale and completely free — one of the best-value evenings in the region. Closing in under two weeks.

Insider tip: About an hour by car from Stuttgart, or an ICE to Karlsruhe Hbf then the tram. Shows start after dark, around 21:00 in early September — plan for a late night home, and bring something to sit on since the lawn fills up.

🦖 Dinosaurs — In the Realm of Prehistory, Karlsruhe

Karlsruhe · Running now · Ticketed

A touring exhibition of around 50 life-size animatronic dinosaurs with movement and sound, built for walking through rather than reading about. Squarely aimed at the elementary-school end of the age range, and reliably a hit with that group.

Insider tip: Check the tour dates page before committing — this is a travelling show and the Karlsruhe run is finite. Pair it with the Schlosslichtspiele for a single Karlsruhe day trip: dinosaurs in the afternoon, projections after dark.

Long-running — worth planning now

🎃 Kürbisausstellung — Ludwigsburg Pumpkin Festival

Blühendes Barock, Ludwigsburg · Through Sun 1 Nov · Ticketed (park admission)

The largest pumpkin festival in the world, staged in the baroque gardens of Ludwigsburg Residential Palace: enormous sculptural displays built from tens of thousands of squash, a giant-pumpkin weigh-off, pumpkin carving, and a food operation that puts pumpkin into everything including the Spätzle. The gardens themselves are excellent, so the ticket covers a full afternoon regardless.

Insider tip: Go on a weekday if you can — weekends in October are genuinely crowded. Same S4/S5 to Ludwigsburg as the Venetian Fair, then bus or a 20-minute walk. The regatta and weigh-off events are the ones worth timing a visit around.
