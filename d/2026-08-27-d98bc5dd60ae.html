Taiwan reveals Chiang Kung hypersonic interceptor — the outer layer of the T-Dome concept

Taiwan's National Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology disclosed specifications for the Chiang Kung (Tien Kung IV) on Aug. 22: a road-mobile vertical launcher holding four missiles, a two-stage solid-fuel rocket with thrust-vector control, and intercept altitude up to 70 km — against 45 km for the Tien Kung III. So WhatAn indigenous mid-tier interceptor reduces Taiwan's dependence on US Patriot deliveries and reshapes the magazine-depth problem in a Strait contingency. On This Day in History

August 27, 1776 — Battle of Long Island. The largest battle of the Revolutionary War. Washington's Continental Army was outflanked and defeated on Brooklyn's heights, then executed a night withdrawal of some 9,000 troops across the East River under fog without losing a man — a retrograde operation still taught as a model of disciplined disengagement.
August 27, 1928 — Kellogg-Briand Pact signed in Paris. Fifteen nations, eventually more than sixty, formally renounced war as an instrument of national policy. It failed to prevent the Second World War, but its language became the legal foundation for the crime of aggressive war charged at Nuremberg.
