Defense One · 8 Sep 2026

RAND: the US may lack the engineers to rebuild space assets in a future war

Industry is already short of the skilled engineering personnel that would be needed to reconstitute satellites lost in a conflict, according to new RAND analysis. Reconstitution timelines in space plans generally assume manufacturing capacity is the binding constraint; RAND's finding is that human capital binds first, and cannot be surged the way a production line can. The shortfall compounds with the launch-cadence and supply-chain pressures already in the national security space enterprise.

So WhatAny theatre plan that assumes degraded space capability will be restored inside a campaign timeline should be stress-tested against a much longer reconstitution assumption. Confirm your PACE plans for SATCOM, PNT and missile warning survive a months-long, not weeks-long, outage.

IISS Strategic Comments · 7 Sep 2026

Fortification returns to NATO's eastern flank — but the concrete is ahead of the concept

Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and Poland are building prepared defensive infrastructure at a scale unseen since the Cold War: up to 600 hardened bunkers on Estonia's Russian border designed to survive 152mm fire, Latvia's €303m counter-mobility plan, Lithuania's 50 engineering-equipment parks and roughly €1.1bn commitment, and Poland's East Shield across some 700km of the Belarus and Kaliningrad frontier at about $2.7bn through 2028. IISS judges the operational logic sound — deterrence by denial, buying time for mobilisation and Article 5 reinforcement — but finds no public evidence the works have been assigned to formations, covered by fires or exercised alongside reinforcement routes. Ukraine's experience also cuts against fixed positions: the drone-driven kill zone along the contact line has deepened from 5–10km to an assessed 35–40km, pushing defenders toward smaller, dispersed, concealed strongpoints.

So WhatThe obstacle belts only work if friendly passage, obstacle coverage and reinforcement routes are rehearsed, and IISS says that rehearsal is the missing piece — which is exactly the seam a US operational headquarters can close. Push for passage-control and obstacle-coverage play in the next reinforcement exercise rather than treating the Baltic Defence Line as someone else's engineering project.

🍺 Feuerbacher Kirbe 2026

Festplatz Feuerbach, Stuttgart · 10–14 September · Free entry

Feuerbach's traditional five-day parish fair — live music, food stalls, beer, fairground rides and games in a working Stuttgart neighbourhood rather than a tourist setting. 🏛️ Tag des offenen Denkmals — Open Monument Day

Across Stuttgart, Baden-Württemberg and all of Germany · Sunday 13 September · Almost all free

Once a year, historic buildings that are normally closed to the public open their doors with tours, talks and special exhibitions — industrial monuments, private houses, church towers, bunkers and mills among them. 🎃 Kürbisausstellung — Ludwigsburg Pumpkin Festival

Blühendes Barock, Ludwigsburg Residential Palace · 22 August – 1 November · Palace gardens ticket

The largest pumpkin festival in the world, staged in the baroque gardens of Ludwigsburg Palace: enormous themed sculpture displays built from hundreds of thousands of squashes, a giant-pumpkin weigh-off, pumpkin soup and Spätzle, and a huge adjoining playground. It runs for ten weeks, so there is no urgency — but it is markedly less crowded in September than during October half-term. The palace gardens ticket covers the fairytale garden too, which absorbs younger children for hours.

Insider tip: 20–25 minutes by car from Panzer, or S4/S5 to Ludwigsburg and a 15-minute walk. Go on a September weekday afternoon if you can; October weekends are genuinely packed.

🎡 Cannstatter Volksfest

Cannstatter Wasen, Bad Cannstatt · 25 September – 11 October · Free entry, pay per ride/tent

Europe's second-largest beer festival after Munich's Oktoberfest, and the defining event of the Stuttgart autumn: seven large tents, a full fairground, the fruit column, and a parade on the opening weekend. It is two very different festivals depending on when you go — daytime and early evening is a family fairground with rides, roast almonds and hearty food, while the tents after dark are a proper beer hall. Opening weekend and the first Saturday are the busiest of the entire run.

Insider tip: S-Bahn or U-Bahn to Bad Cannstatt / Mercedesstraße and do not attempt to drive or park. If you want a tent table with a group, reserve now — tables for the good slots are largely gone by mid-September. Take children in the afternoon.
