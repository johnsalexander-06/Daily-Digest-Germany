ISW: Russia's second Rassvet satellite batch failed to reach operational altitude

None of the 16 Rassvet satellites launched on 19 July have reached their intended 550km altitude, with many stuck between 300 and 400km, some already decaying and at least two heading for reentry — well short of the 870km operational target. Only 12 of the first 16, launched in March, made their intended altitude, putting the overall success rate at 37.5 percent. Russia is rushing the constellation to replace the Starlink access it lost on 1 February, but it depends on the scarce Soyuz-2.1b launcher and has managed only six to eight such launches since 2022. Ukrainian advisors assess Russia needs 200-300 satellites for stable coverage; at current tempo it could field at most 128 in four years.

So WhatRussian satellite communications over Ukraine will stay intermittent rather than continuous for the near term, which keeps a ceiling on remotely-piloted strike coordination — but the existing 12 satellites already give roughly 90 minutes of connectivity a day. Treat Russian space-based C2 as degraded and windowed rather than absent, and expect activity to cluster inside those windows.

🏙️ What To Do — Stuttgart & Baden-Württemberg

Friday 4 – Sunday 6 September
🎭 Venezianische Messe (Venetian Fair) — Ludwigsburg city centre · 4–6 Sep · Mostly free to walk
Ludwigsburg's Venetian Fair is held only every two years, turning the baroque city centre into a Venetian carnival with masked costume processions, street performers, music and food stalls across the whole weekend. It is the single biggest thing happening in the local area this month and the next one is not until 2028.
Insider tip: about 20 minutes north on the S4/S5, and worth taking the train — Ludwigsburg parking is hopeless on fair weekends. Costumed performers are out in the afternoon, which is the part younger kids actually care about; go Saturday afternoon rather than Saturday night.
Running now — through 5 September
🍷 Stuttgarter Weindorf — Marktplatz & Schillerplatz, downtown Stuttgart · final weekend, through 5 Sep · Free entry
The city's signature late-summer wine village is in its closing days: around 120 growers pouring more than 500 Württemberg wines from timbered booths across the old town squares, with full Swabian kitchens serving Maultaschen, Zwiebelrostbraten and Käsespätzle. Entry is free; you pay per glass and per plate.
Insider tip: last chance until next year. A few hundred metres from the Hauptbahnhof, so take the Bahn. Before about 18:00 the squares are relaxed and it reads as a food festival; after dark it fills up and becomes a drinking crowd — pick your hour accordingly.
Running now — through 13 September
🍝 Stuttgart Sicilian Fest — Pariser Platz, Stuttgart · through 13 Sep · Free entry
Two weeks of Sicilian street food, craft beer and live entertainment at Pariser Platz. Smaller and considerably more relaxed than Weindorf, which makes it the easier option with children in tow.
Insider tip: Pariser Platz is directly on the U-Bahn, and because the fest runs on weekdays too you can go on a quiet evening rather than fighting the weekend crowd.
💡 Schlosslichtspiele — Karlsruhe Palace Light Festival — Karlsruhe Palace · nightly after dark · Free
Large-scale projection mapping across the full face of Karlsruhe Palace, this year under the motto "Lightyard Stories: Visions of Urban Living Spaces." Free to watch from the palace lawn.
Insider tip: roughly an hour west on the A8 and free at the gate. Shows do not start until true dark — closer to 21:00 in September — so plan a late dinner in Karlsruhe first rather than arriving early with restless kids.
Every Sunday — through 11 October
🚗 Classics and Coffee — Mercedes-Benz Museum, Bad Cannstatt · Sunday mornings · Free to attend
Weekly open-air gathering of classic and collector cars in the museum forecourt, every Sunday through 11 October. Attending the meet-up costs nothing; the museum interior is a separate paid ticket.
Insider tip: park once and do both — the outdoor meet in the morning, then the museum when the crowd thins. Easy S-Bahn to Neckarpark if you would rather not drive into Bad Cannstatt on a Sunday.
Every Saturday & Sunday
🌍 Free Walking Tour in English — Königstraße 1a · Sat & Sun, 11:00 · Free (tip-based)
Two-hour English-language walking tour of the city centre, departing from Königstraße behind a guide carrying a yellow umbrella. No booking required.
Insider tip: the highest-value two hours on this list for a recently PCS'd family — it orients everyone to downtown, the Bahn and Schlossplatz at once. Two hours on foot is a stretch for the youngest end of the range, so bring water and plan an exit.
Running now — through 1 November
🎃 Kürbisausstellung — Ludwigsburg Pumpkin Festival — Blühendes Barock, Ludwigsburg · through 1 Nov · Ticketed (garden admission)
The largest pumpkin festival in the world, in the baroque gardens of Ludwigsburg Residential Palace: themed carving displays, pumpkin dishes and a fairy-tale garden aimed at younger children. Garden admission covers the exhibition and the surrounding playgrounds.
Insider tip: the best all-ages outing on this list and the closest to post. If you are already going to the Venetian Fair on 4–6 September, note both are in Ludwigsburg — but do them on separate days, as the fair crowd makes the gardens unpleasant.
