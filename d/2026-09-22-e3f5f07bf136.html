Pentagon review faults stale imagery and AI overreliance in the Minab school strike

Pentagon investigators found that flawed intelligence, outdated imagery and overreliance on AI contributed to the 28 February US missile strike on a school in Minab, Iran. Two Tomahawks hit the building, killing more than 150 people including at least 123 children. Some Pentagon personnel reportedly knew within hours that the strike was American. The finding lands three days after a UN mission concluded there were reasonable grounds to treat US strikes in Iran that killed 178 civilians as war crimes, and it supplies a mechanism for what that mission described only as an outcome.

So WhatThis is the first official US account attributing a mass-casualty targeting failure in part to automated support, and it will be read as precedent wherever AI-assisted targeting is being fielded. Commanders should expect their own ISR-to-target chains to be scrutinised against this finding: specifically, imagery currency standards, the named human authority that certifies a target's civilian status, and whether an analyst can actually override a machine-generated recommendation in the time available. Review those three now rather than after they are asked for.

The reason it belongs in a European inbox is that the same logic — allied restraint contingent on a peer ally's choice rather than on American guarantees — is the structure of the European nuclear debate too, and a South Korean decision would move the conversation in Berlin and Warsaw as well as Tokyo.
ASPI · 21 Sep

ASPI maps 19,000 Chinese "Little Giants" in critical technologies

The Australian Strategic Policy Institute launched a China Little Giants Tracker mapping more than 19,000 Chinese companies assessed as capable of occupying leading positions in critical technologies and industries. The value is granularity: the tracker moves the discussion from national champions to the middle tier of specialised firms where supply-chain dependencies actually form.

This week — 22 to 28 September

🎉 Cannstatter VolksfestOpens Friday

Cannstatter Wasen, Bad Cannstatt · 25 September – 11 October · free entry; pay per ride, food and tent

Europe's second-largest beer festival after Munich's Oktoberfest, running seventeen days on the Wasen. Beyond the tents there is a full fairground of carnival rides, hearty Swabian food and regional producers, and the Fruchtsäule — the fruit column at the centre of the grounds — is the traditional focal point. The character changes sharply by time of day: afternoons are a family fairground, evenings inside the tents are a loud, standing-on-benches drinking hall.

Insider tip: go between opening and about 18:00 with children — the rides are running, the tents are still seated and conversational, and queues are shortest. S-Bahn to Bad Cannstatt (S1, S2 or S3) and walk; do not attempt to park on the grounds, and expect trains back into the city to be crowded and loud after dark.

🎃 Kürbisausstellung — Pumpkin Festival Ludwigsburg

Blühendes Barock, Ludwigsburg Residential Palace · through 1 November · Blühendes Barock admission

Billed as the world's largest pumpkin exhibition, set in the baroque gardens of the Ludwigsburg palace with enormous sculptural displays, carving, pumpkin-based food stalls and a giant-pumpkin weigh-off. It is the strongest single outing on this list for families with school-age children because the pumpkin displays and the gardens work for a wide age spread at once.

Insider tip: your Blühendes Barock ticket also covers the Märchengarten, the fairy-tale garden on the same grounds — budget an extra ninety minutes for it with anyone under about twelve. S4 or S5 to Ludwigsburg then a short bus or roughly twenty-minute walk; by car it is about 25 minutes from central Stuttgart, and weekday afternoons are far quieter than October weekends.

🚶 Free Walking Tour in English

Meets Königstrasse 1a, central Stuttgart · Saturdays and Sundays, 11:00 · free, tip-based

A two-hour walking tour of the city centre in English, with the guide identifiable by a yellow umbrella. For a newly arrived family this is the cheapest way to convert a confusing downtown into a mental map — Schlossplatz, the Altes and Neues Schloss, and the Markthalle area all sit within the loop.

Insider tip: best used in the first month after arrival, and it pairs naturally with the Karlsplatz flea market a few minutes away on a Saturday. It is genuinely free but guides work for tips, so bring small cash; two hours on foot is a stretch for under-sixes.

🛍️ Karlsplatz Flea Market

Karlsplatz, Stuttgart Altstadt · every Saturday · free to browse

A weekly flea market in the old town square beside the Neues Schloss, heavy on porcelain, glassware, tools, books and genuine junk. It is the standard place for arriving families to pick up the household oddments that never survive a PCS, and haggling in cash is expected rather than rude.

Insider tip: arrive before about 10:00 for anything worth buying and bring small notes — almost nobody takes cards. Walk in from Schlossplatz or Charlottenplatz rather than driving; parking in the Altstadt on a Saturday is not worth the attempt.

🚗 Classics and Coffee at the Mercedes-Benz MuseumEnds 11 Oct

Mercedes-Benz Museum, Stuttgart-Untertürkheim · every Sunday through 11 October · museum admission

Classic cars on show with coffee on Sunday mornings, running only until 11 October, so there are three Sundays left in this window. The museum itself is a nine-floor spiral that is reliably a hit with children who have no interest whatsoever in cars, which makes it a safe wet-weather fallback.

🍷 Fellbacher Herbst — Wine and Harvest FestivalOne weekend only

Marktplatz, Fellbach · 9–12 October · free entry

Fellbach's harvest festival, held the second weekend of October, with local wine tastings from the surrounding vineyards, fairground rides, a parade and a fireworks show. It is a markedly smaller and more local affair than the Volksfest — a town festival rather than a regional one — which is exactly its appeal if the Wasen feels like too much.

Insider tip: S2 or S3 to Fellbach and walk to the Marktplatz; the vineyard slopes above the town are worth an hour on foot before the festival if the weather holds. The source lists a parade and fireworks but does not publish day-by-day times, so check the Fellbach town site before committing a specific afternoon.

A Florida charter boat reeled in a drone, and Eglin admitted losing one

Air & Space Forces Magazine, commentary by Richard P. Hallion · 21 Sep

An F-117 Nighthawk is finally going to the National Air and Space Museum

The Smithsonian has announced that F-117A tail number 85-0818 is headed to the National Air and Space Museum. Hallion's argument is that the acquisition fills a long-standing gap in the collection of what is generally considered the world's premier air and space museum: the aircraft that made low observability an operational reality rather than a laboratory claim has, until now, been absent from the national collection.
