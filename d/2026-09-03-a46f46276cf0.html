Continuing resolution freezes DoD at FY26 levels, parking a $1.5T budget request

With 30 days left in fiscal 2026, Congress passed a CR on Sept. 1 extending current spending through Dec. 11 and putting the Pentagon's $1.5 trillion FY27 spending authority request on hold. Under a CR, new-start programs cannot begin and production rates cannot increase without specific anomalies. The measure also temporarily extends the lapsed cyber information-sharing statute to the same Dec. 11 date, pushing two significant deadlines onto the same week in December.

So WhatAnything in the FY27 request — new starts, ramp-ups, MILCON at European installations — is frozen for at least 100 days, and a December cliff now stacks appropriations and cyber authorities together. Staff should re-baseline execution plans to FY26 rates now and identify which requirements will break if the CR is extended past Dec. 11.

Hezbollah's FPV drone program is an acute and near-term problem for Israel

Hezbollah is learning from its past military failures and building out first-person-view drone capability for current and future campaigns against Israel, according to ISW's assessment. FPV drones give a non-state actor precision effects at a fraction of the cost of guided munitions and are extremely difficult to counter at scale with conventional air defense. The development mirrors the tactical evolution seen in Ukraine, now transferred into the Levant.

So WhatCheap precision drone capability is diffusing from Ukraine into non-state actors faster than counter-UAS fielding, and the same TTPs will reach any adversary proxy operating near U.S. installations. Force protection planners should assume FPV attack is a current threat rather than an emerging one, and prioritize detection and passive defense over interceptor-based solutions.

Axios AI+ / Washington Post AI & Tech Brief · Sept. 2, 2026

Frontier labs are trimming model chain-of-thought — and not fixing known safety failures

Reporting this week describes a race to the bottom in cutting how much of a model's chain of thought is exposed, alongside a broader disconnect between what frontier labs say about safety and what they are actually correcting. Reduced reasoning transparency makes it harder for any downstream user — including government customers — to audit why a model produced a given output. The commercial incentive is cost and latency; the cost is verifiability.

So WhatIf reasoning traces disappear, AI-assisted analytic products become harder to defend in a decision brief and harder to reconstruct after the fact. Insist that any AI tool used in staff work retains an auditable trail, and do not let model output enter an assessment without a named human owner.

Friday 4 – Sunday 6 September

🎭 Venetianische Messe — Venetian Fair, Ludwigsburg

Ludwigsburg city center · Fri 4 – Sun 6 Sept · free to walk the fair

Ludwigsburg's baroque old town is taken over by costumed Venetian carnival figures, street performers, masked processions and stalls, all set against the palace district. This one is biennial — it only runs every two years, so if you skip it you are waiting until 2028. It is a genuinely visual event that works for kids who would be bored by a normal town fest.

Insider tip: about 20 minutes north of Stuttgart; take S4 or S5 to Ludwigsburg and walk rather than fighting for parking near the Marktplatz. Late afternoon is the sweet spot — costumes are out in force and the light is good for photos, before the evening crowd thickens.

🍷 Stuttgarter Weindorf — Wine Village

Marktplatz, Schillerplatz & Kirchstraße, downtown Stuttgart · through Sat 5 Sept · free entry

Around 125 regional vineyards set up wooden arbors through the city center pouring more than 500 Württemberg wines alongside proper Swabian cooking — Maultaschen, Zwiebelrostbraten, Käsespätzle. Closing this weekend, so this is the last chance until next August.

Insider tip: go for lunch or early evening with children — the arbors function as outdoor restaurants and are family-friendly until roughly 20:00, after which it becomes a drinking crowd. S-Bahn to Stuttgart Hauptbahnhof or Schlossplatz; do not drive downtown.

Running now — through 13 September

🍝 Stuttgart Sicilian Fest

Pariser Platz, Stuttgart · through Sun 13 Sept · free entry

Sicilian street food, cannoli, arancini, craft beer and live entertainment in an open square near the city center. Smaller and less crowded than the Weindorf, and an easy evening if you want the fest atmosphere without the scale.

Insider tip: Pariser Platz sits by the Feuersee/Stadtmitte area — reachable on the U-Bahn in minutes from the Hauptbahnhof. Weeknights are noticeably calmer than weekends if you are bringing younger children.

💡 Schlosslichtspiele — Palace Light Festival, Karlsruhe

Karlsruhe Palace façade · nightly through Sun 13 Sept · free

Large-scale projection mapping turns the entire baroque palace front into a moving canvas, this year under the motto "Lightyard Stories: Visions of Urban Living Spaces." It is free, outdoors, and genuinely spectacular — one of the better no-cost family outings in the region, and it ends in ten days.

Insider tip: roughly one hour by car or ICE from Stuttgart. Shows start at dusk, so around 20:30 in early September; arrive 30 minutes early for a spot on the lawn and bring a blanket — the grass is cold by the second show.

🦕 Dinosaurs — In the Realm of Prehistory, Karlsruhe

Karlsruhe · check site for daily hours · ticketed

Fifty life-sized animatronic and model dinosaurs in a walk-through exhibition built squarely for kids, with the usual mix of skeletons, sound and scale models. Pairs naturally with the Schlosslichtspiele if you make Karlsruhe a single day trip — exhibition in the afternoon, projections after dark.

Insider tip: best for roughly ages 4–12; older teens will burn through it in 45 minutes. Confirm the exact venue and hours on the tour date page before setting out, since this is a traveling exhibition.

All month — through 1 November

🎃 Kürbisausstellung — Ludwigsburg Pumpkin Festival

Blühendes Barock gardens, Ludwigsburg Residential Palace · through Sun 1 Nov · garden admission

The largest pumpkin exhibition in the world, staged in the palace gardens: enormous themed sculptures built from pumpkins, a carving program, pumpkin soup and Maultaschen stands, and a giant-pumpkin weigh-off later in the season. Ticket includes the Blühendes Barock gardens and the adjacent fairy-tale garden, which is what actually holds younger children for an afternoon.

Insider tip: go on a weekday morning — weekends in October are genuinely packed. Same S4/S5 run to Ludwigsburg; the gardens are a 10-minute walk from the station and the palace ticket is good for the whole day, so you can leave for lunch and come back.

Mercedes-Benz Museum, Bad Cannstatt · Sundays through 11 Oct · free to attend the meet

Owners bring classic Mercedes and other collector cars to the museum forecourt on Sunday mornings; you can walk the rows, talk to owners and get coffee without paying museum admission. A low-commitment Sunday that works for teenagers who have run out of patience for castles.
