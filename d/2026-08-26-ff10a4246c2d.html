ISW China & Taiwan Update · Aug 25

Taiwan reveals "Chiang Kung" hypersonic interceptor as outer layer of T-Dome

Taiwan's National Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology disclosed specifications on Aug. 22 for the Chiang Kung (Strong Bow), also designated Tien Kung IV. The land-based mobile vertical launch system fires interceptors at hypersonic speeds against ballistic missiles at altitudes up to 70 km — a major increase over the Tien Kung III's 45 km ceiling — using a two-stage solid-fuel rocket with thrust-vector control, four missiles per launcher, and a mobile AESA radar vehicle co-developed with the United States. Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense plans two systems with at least 128 missiles for roughly $1.13 billion, forming the outermost layer of President Lai's T-Dome integrated air and missile defense concept alongside Tien Kung III and PAC-3. Legislative gridlock remains the binding constraint: the special defense budget passed in May excluded funding for the missile.

So WhatIndigenous upper-tier interception would materially reduce Taiwan's dependence on US Patriot deliveries and change the PLA's missile-salvo calculus — if the money ever appears. Track the Legislative Yuan budget fight as the real indicator, not the technical reveal.

Futurum Group via TLDR InfoSec · Aug 25

Why AI learned to attack before it learned to defend

Offensive AI is outpacing defensive AI because of a structural asymmetry: an exploit either lands or it does not, giving reinforcement learning a clean verification signal that defense lacks. The imbalance is already visible — Anthropic's Project Glasswing surfaced over 10,000 high- or critical-severity flaws, far outpacing the roughly two-week average patch time, while OpenAI's Daybreak Red reached about a 95 percent success rate on advanced offensive security tasks. The July Hugging Face breach and the spread of highly capable open-weight models compound the effect. AI substitutes for human effort on offense but only assists on defense.

So WhatYour patch cycle is measured in weeks while machine-generated vulnerability discovery is measured in hours, and that gap is widening rather than closing. Push your cyber defenders toward automating the full loop — detection through patch, deploy and rollback — because manual remediation cadence will not hold.
