Institute for the Study of War · 6 Sep 2026

Russia's culture of "beautiful reports" is corrupting Putin's own picture of the war

Kremlin aide Yuriy Ushakov said Putin gave US envoys a comprehensive assessment of "significant advances" along the front — a characterization that does not match conditions on the ground. ISW attributes this to the pervasive practice within the Russian armed forces that milbloggers call "beautiful reports," compounded by senior commanders overstating gains as part of a deliberate cognitive-warfare effort. The result is a leader hardening his belief that he can take the rest of Donetsk Oblast by the end of 2026 and therefore need concede nothing.

So WhatAn adversary who is misinformed about his own battlefield position is harder to deter than one who is merely aggressive, because the signals we send are being filtered through a false baseline. Build indications-and-warning products around what Russian forces can actually do, not around what Russian leadership says it is doing, and expect negotiation timelines to slip accordingly.

🍝 Stuttgart Sicilian Fest

Pariser Platz, Stuttgart · through 13 September · Free entry, pay per item

A Sicilian street-food festival in the middle of the city, with craft beer, live entertainment and stalls running through the second week of September. It is small enough to do in ninety minutes and central enough that you can pair it with something else downtown. Arancini and cannoli do most of the heavy lifting.

Insider tip: Pariser Platz is directly on the S-Bahn at Stuttgart Nord/Feuerbach side of the city — take the train rather than fight for parking. Go before 18:00 with children; it turns into an adult evening crowd after dark.

✨ Schlosslichtspiele — Karlsruhe Palace Light Festival

Karlsruhe Palace · nightly through 13 September · Free

Large-scale projection mapping onto the whole facade of Karlsruhe Palace, this year themed "Lightyard Stories: Visions of Urban Living Spaces." The show runs in a loop across the evening, the lawn in front is open and free, and people bring blankets and picnics. Closing for the year on 13 September — this is the last week.

Insider tip: about an hour by car from Stuttgart, or an hour by train to Karlsruhe Hbf plus a short tram ride. Shows start once it is properly dark, around 20:30 in mid-September, so plan on a late night home with kids.

Weekends through the autumn

🦖 Kürbisausstellung — Ludwigsburg Pumpkin Festival

Blühendes Barock, Ludwigsburg · through 1 November · Palace gardens admission

The largest pumpkin festival in the world, held on the grounds of the Ludwigsburg Residential Palace. Enormous carved displays built to an annual theme, pumpkin soup and pumpkin spätzle everywhere, and a full children's playground in the Blühendes Barock gardens that makes the ticket worth it on its own. The pumpkin weigh-off and regatta events land later in the season.

Insider tip: S4/S5 to Ludwigsburg then a fifteen-minute walk, or twenty-five minutes by car from Panzer. Go on a weekday afternoon if you can — September weekends here are genuinely crowded, and the garden ticket covers the whole afternoon.

🚗 Classics & Coffee at the Mercedes-Benz Museum

Mercedes-Benz Museum, Bad Cannstatt · Sundays through 11 October · Free to attend the meet

Every Sunday morning through mid-October, classic car owners gather on the museum plaza and the public wanders through with coffee. You do not need a museum ticket to walk the meet, which makes it one of the better free mornings in the city for anyone with a car-obsessed ten-year-old. The museum itself is worth the separate admission if you have not done it yet.

Insider tip: U-Bahn to Neckarpark or park in the museum garage. Arrive by 09:30 — the best cars come early and the plaza thins out by late morning.

🦕 Dinosaurs — In the Realm of Prehistory

Karlsruhe · check current dates on site · Ticketed

A travelling exhibition of roughly fifty life-sized animatronic dinosaurs, aimed squarely at elementary and middle-school children. It pairs naturally with the Palace Light Festival if you are already making the Karlsruhe drive this week — exhibition in the afternoon, projections after dark.

Insider tip: confirm the current Karlsruhe dates on the tour schedule page before you go, since this show moves between cities. Roughly an hour from Stuttgart on the A8/A5.

🚶 Free Walking Tour of Stuttgart, in English

Königstrasse 1a · Saturdays and Sundays, 11:00 · Free, tip-based

A two-hour English-language walking tour of the city centre, departing from Königstrasse and led by a guide carrying a yellow umbrella. It is the single best orientation to Stuttgart for a newly arrived family, and it costs whatever you decide to tip at the end. Good for teenagers, long for anyone under about eight.

Insider tip: no booking required — just show up at the meeting point a few minutes early. Pair it with the Markthalle a few blocks away for lunch afterwards.

Opens 25 September — plan ahead

🍺 Cannstatter Volksfest

Cannstatter Wasen, Bad Cannstatt · 25 September – 11 October · Free entry, pay for rides and food

The second-largest beer festival in Europe after Munich's Oktoberfest, and the defining Stuttgart event of the year. Alongside the tents there is a full fairground — a large Ferris wheel, roller coasters and the traditional stalls — plus the opening parade and a fireworks programme. It is a genuinely different event by time of day: an afternoon fairground for families, a very different scene in the tents after dark.

Insider tip: go in the afternoon with children and use the family discount days early in the run. Take the U-Bahn to Cannstatter Wasen — parking is effectively impossible — and if you want a tent table on a weekend, reserve now rather than in October.

History Facts · 6 Sep 2026

The ration line that ended up in everyone's pantry

A number of foods now firmly civilian started life as a quartermaster's problem to solve. M&Ms were developed with a hard candy shell so troops could carry chocolate through heat without it melting in their pockets — a packaging fix that outlived the war and became a household brand. The same story repeats across the shelf: things invented to keep soldiers fed in bad conditions turned out to be things everyone wanted.
