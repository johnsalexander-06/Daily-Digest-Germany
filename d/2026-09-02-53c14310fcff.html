Institute for the Study of War · 31 Aug 2026

Russia's second Rassvet satellite batch fails to reach operational altitude

None of the 16 Rassvet satellites launched on 19 July have reached their anticipated 550km altitude as of 31 August, according to N2YO tracking data; many settled between 300 and 400km, some are experiencing orbital decay, and at least two are reportedly heading for reentry. The intended operational altitude is 870km. Of the first batch launched 24 March, only 12 of 16 reached sustainable altitude — a 37.5 percent overall success rate — and those provide Russian forces roughly two connectivity windows totalling up to 90 minutes per day over Ukraine. Moscow accelerated the program after losing Starlink access on 1 February. Ukrainian advisor Serhiy Beskrestnov assesses Russia needs 200–300 satellites for stable 24/7 coverage; Russia has flown its Soyuz-2.1b medium-lift vehicle only six to eight times since 2022, capping realistic deployment near 128 satellites over four years. ISW separately assesses Putin favors covert mobilization after the 18–20 September State Duma elections rather than a formal call-up.

So WhatRussian battlefield C2 and precision-strike enablement will remain intermittent and predictable — those 90-minute daily windows are an exploitable pattern, not a capability. Track the 18–20 September Duma elections as the trigger date for the next Russian force-generation decision, and treat any post-election mobilization step as the signal for a revised manpower picture rather than an immediate posture change.

🍷 Stuttgarter Weindorf — Wine Village

Marktplatz & Schillerplatz, downtown Stuttgart · through 5 September · free entry, pay per glass

The city centre fills with wooden wine arbours from around 125 regional vineyards pouring more than 500 Württemberg wines, alongside full Swabian kitchens — Maultaschen, Zwiebelrostbraten, Käsespätzle. It is one of the two defining Stuttgart events of the year and it ends this Saturday. Daytime and early evening it functions as an outdoor restaurant with families at long shared tables; after roughly 20:00 the character shifts decisively toward an adult drinking crowd and the aisles get tight.

Insider tip: Go for an early dinner around 17:00 with kids — you will get a table, the food service is faster, and you will be gone before the crush. S-Bahn to Stadtmitte or Hauptbahnhof; do not attempt to park downtown.

🎭 Venezianische Messe — Venetian Fair, Ludwigsburg

Ludwigsburg city centre · Friday 4 – Sunday 6 September · free to watch

Held only every two years — this is the rare one. Ludwigsburg's baroque centre is taken over by hundreds of costumed performers in Venetian carnival masks and 18th-century dress, with commedia dell'arte street theatre, processions and music staged among the palace-era buildings. It is genuinely spectacular for photographs and works well for children old enough to walk a few hours, who tend to be transfixed by the masks.

Insider tip: If you miss it this weekend the next one is 2028. About 20 minutes by S-Bahn (S4/S5) from Stuttgart Hbf to Ludwigsburg, then a short walk — far easier than driving, since the centre is closed and the parking decks fill by late morning. Best light for photos is the last two hours before dusk.
