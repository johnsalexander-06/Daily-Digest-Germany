Defense One · Aug 6

Seasats bets the Marine Corps and Navy fight on small unmanned surface vessels

Seasats CEO Mike Flanigan says the company deliberately won't build ship-sized drone boats, concentrating instead on small USVs: the 12-foot Lightfish (95% of its business), the 20-foot Quickfish — a "reactionary tool" to reposition assets or intercept targets — and the 35-foot Heavyfish debuting later this year. A Lightfish recently captured surveillance footage of a Chinese warship near the Philippines, and another crossed the Pacific from San Diego to Japan autonomously. The San Diego firm is supporting Marine Corps, Navy, and Special Forces programs and expanding internationally.

So What: Attritable, small USVs are already delivering ISR value in the first island chain and the market is maturing fast. Fold autonomous surface ISR into maritime-domain-awareness and force-protection planning rather than treating unmanned surface as a future capability.

Defense One · Aug 6

CBO: Trump's nuclear battleship could be the most expensive US warship ever

The Congressional Budget Office estimates the nuclear-powered battleship (BBGN) program would cost about $275 billion, with the first ship near $23.4 billion — up sharply from the administration's original $15.1 billion estimate — falling to roughly $18 billion per ship across a 15-ship fleet built through 2056. Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-Ore.), ranking member of the Senate Budget Committee, ordered the analysis. For comparison, a DDG-51 destroyer runs more than $3 billion, so the Navy could buy five destroyers for the price of one battleship — though the destroyers carry higher crewing and fuel costs.

So What: These numbers reopen a fleet-architecture fight likely to surface in the FY27 NDAA. Expect budget pressure to ripple into shipbuilding and readiness accounts, and watch for trade-offs against cheaper distributed and unmanned options.

Politico NatSec Daily · Aug 6

Skepticism greets claims of a US–China military thaw

The Pentagon insists military-to-military ties with China are "stronger than they have been in years," with officials crediting Secretary Hegseth for steady repair of a once-broken channel. Former Indo-Pacific Pentagon officials call that "propaganda or marketing," noting the resumed operational-level dialogues predate this administration. Beijing is ignoring policy chief Elbridge Colby's push for an official visit, and the PLA's ongoing senior-ranks purge complicates high-level contact. Retired ONI chief Mike Studeman warns Beijing historically uses such engagements for one-sided information gathering and truncates them whenever rivalry re-intensifies.

So What: Don't mistake resumed talking for real crisis-communications capacity. INDOPACOM commanders should assume deconfliction channels remain thin and plan for ambiguity — and delayed responses from Beijing — in a fast-moving incident.

ISW · Aug 6

Lithuania warns of a possible Russian false-flag using seized Ukrainian drones

Lithuanian Defense Minister Robertas Kaunas warned on August 6 that Russia may use Ukrainian-manufactured drones to stage a false-flag operation against the Baltic states, echoing a July 21 warning from Poland's defense minister. Ukrainian sources report Russia may have already begun using seized long-range drones for provocations. ISW assesses this fits a Russian "Phase Zero" campaign — false flags, drone incursions, sabotage, electronic-warfare interference, and overflights — designed to set conditions for future provocations and test NATO's response. Latvia says it sees no indicators of imminent aggression against its critical infrastructure.

So What: Attribution ambiguity is the objective — a Ukrainian-marked drone over the Baltics could be engineered to fracture NATO consensus. Eastern-flank commanders should pre-coordinate attribution and response protocols now, before an incident forces a rushed decision.

Russia forms a combat unit from Ukrainian POWs — a Geneva Convention violation

Russia's Ministry of Defense Volunteer Corps announced on August 6 the formation of a "Ukrainian Volunteer Spetsnaz Brigade," merging irregular units reportedly comprising Ukrainian prisoners of war and servicemembers who allegedly defected to Russia. At least one Russian milblogger openly acknowledged the brigade includes POWs — striking given that using POWs in combat is a war crime. ISW first observed such Russian practices in 2022.

So What: This extends a documented pattern of Russian POW abuse and information manipulation, strengthening the legal and information-operations case against Moscow and signaling how it may exploit coerced manpower as attrition mounts.

TLDR Cybersecurity · Aug 6

Self-propagating "Shai-Hulud" worm hits the npm software supply chain

A new variant of the Shai-Hulud worm has compromised over 1,280 npm packages — including the widely used Keyv library — by injecting malicious commits. Microsoft separately identified a self-propagating supply-chain attack affecting more than 400 npm packages that steals npm, GitHub, AWS, and Kubernetes credentials as it spreads.

So What: Open-source dependency poisoning is a direct threat to DoD and contractor software pipelines. Confirm SBOM and dependency scanning are enforced, and rotate credentials across CI/CD before compromised packages propagate.
Politico NatSec Daily / Reuters · Aug 6

Iran and Oman near a Strait of Hormuz safe-passage agreement

Iran's Foreign Ministry says Tehran and Oman have agreed on the geographic coordinates for a designated safe-shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, with a joint statement in final review. Spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei cautioned the deal won't guarantee ships' security while "threats and escalations by other countries continue" — a pointed reference to President Trump, who said he called off a fresh wave of strikes over the weekend citing the pending agreement after Gulf allies including Saudi Arabia urged restraint. Reuters reports Tehran had warned Gulf states it would strike their oil, power, and water infrastructure if they failed to steer Trump toward diplomacy.

So What: This is a fragile de-escalation that leaves coercive leverage intact on all sides. CENTCOM planners should treat Hormuz transit as conditionally open and keep escalation-management and convoy-protection options ready to reactivate.
