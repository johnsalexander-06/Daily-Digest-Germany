Interceptor stockpiles run "dangerously low" as Iran war burns through air defenses

The U.S. military has expended roughly four-fifths of its THAAD interceptor inventory and about half its Patriot interceptors since the start of the war with Iran, with commanders warning stockpiles are dangerously low. President Trump reportedly clashed with Defense Secretary Hegseth at Camp David over the shortages, demanding to know why he had not been told earlier that long-range guided munitions and other critical interceptors were running out. The depletion rate now outpaces the industrial base's ability to replenish, exposing a structural mismatch between sustained Middle East air-defense demand and production capacity.

So what: A near-empty interceptor magazine constrains every theater simultaneously — demand pulled to CENTCOM is demand denied to EUCOM and INDOPACOM. Commanders should re-validate their air-and-missile-defense basic loads now and assume munitions resupply timelines will slip; build branch plans that ration high-demand interceptors against only the highest-value threats.
