EUCOM Implications

Fuel is the thread running through three unrelated stories in the last 24 hours. US farm-state Republicans are demanding a diesel export embargo; Macron has asked the Commission to relax EU fuel-quality standards so European refineries can lift jet fuel and diesel output by 5–20%; and both are downstream of Iran-war price spikes with Hormuz and the Red Sea contested at the same time. We judge with moderate confidence that theater bulk-fuel assumptions are now the exposed variable: if Washington restricts diesel exports, European spot supply tightens further; if Brussels relaxes specifications, fuel arriving in theater may not meet the specs our contracts assume. Either path moves sustainment cost and specification risk in the same direction. Worth confirming whether theater fuel contracts carry specification-variance clauses, and what a 20% price swing does to the sustainment line in current plans.

This weekend · 25–27 September

🍺 Cannstatter Volksfest opens

Cannstatter Wasen, Bad Cannstatt · 25 Sep – 11 Oct · free entry, pay per ride and tent

Europe's second-largest Volksfest after Munich, and the one locals will tell you they actually prefer. Beer tents, the full carnival midway, a fruit column landmark, hearty food stands and regional producers. Daytime is a different event from the evening: before about 6 p.m. the tents and midway are full of families and prams, and the rides, not the tents, are the centre of gravity.

Insider tip: take the S-Bahn (S1/S2/S3 to Bad Cannstatt, or U11 which runs direct on fest days) — parking at the Wasen is effectively impossible and the U-Bahn drops you at the gate. Go Saturday or Sunday afternoon with children; opening weekend evenings are the busiest of the entire run.

🏺 Antikmeile — Ludwigsburg Antique Mile

Ludwigsburg town centre · Sat 26 – Sun 27 Sep · free to browse

A street-long antiques and collectables market through the baroque centre of Ludwigsburg. Dealers from across the region set out furniture, prints, glassware, militaria and assorted junk along the pedestrian zone. It pairs naturally with the pumpkin exhibition at the palace fifteen minutes' walk away, which makes it an easy full morning.

Insider tip: about 25 minutes from Patch Barracks by car, or S4/S5 to Ludwigsburg then a short walk. Bring cash — a good number of dealers still do not take cards — and go early if you actually intend to buy.

🦕 Dinosaurs — In the Realm of Prehistory

Ettlingen (west, near Karlsruhe) · all weekend · ticketed

A large walk-through exhibition of life-sized animatronic dinosaurs with accompanying displays on the prehistoric world. Straightforwardly aimed at children and reliably a hit with the elementary-school end of the age range, with enough paleontology in the signage to keep older kids reading.

Insider tip: roughly an hour west by car on the A8 then A5 — this is the one on this list that is a genuine day trip rather than an afternoon. Check the site for timed entry before you set out.

Closing soon · through 4 October

✨ Botanische Lichter — Botanical Lights Tübingen

Botanical Garden, Tübingen · through 4 Oct · ticketed, book ahead

The university botanical garden is lit after dark with illuminated trees and light installations along a walking route through the collections. It is a quiet, slow event rather than a spectacle, which suits younger children and anyone who has had enough of crowds. Closing within the window — 4 October is the last night, and it does not return until next autumn.

Insider tip: about 45 minutes south by car. Tickets are timed and the later slots sell out first; go for an early slot with small children, since full dark plus a long walk back to the car is a familiar mistake here. Dress warmer than the forecast suggests — the garden sits in a river valley.

🚗 Classics and Coffee at the Mercedes-Benz Museum

Mercedes-Benz Museum, Bad Cannstatt · every Sunday through 11 Oct · outdoor gathering free

Owners bring classic and vintage cars to the museum forecourt on Sunday mornings and the public wanders among them with coffee. The outdoor gathering costs nothing; museum admission is separate if you want the collection as well. The season ends 11 October, after which it goes dormant until spring.

Insider tip: go early — the good cars arrive first and start leaving by late morning. Combine it with the Volksfest, which is a ten-minute walk away on the same S-Bahn stop, for a full Sunday without moving the car twice.

Running now · through autumn

🎃 Kürbisausstellung — Ludwigsburg Pumpkin Festival

Blühendes Barock, Ludwigsburg Palace grounds · through 1 Nov · palace grounds admission

The largest pumpkin exhibition in the world, staged across the baroque gardens of Ludwigsburg Residential Palace with enormous themed sculpture displays, carving, a giant-pumpkin weigh-off and a great deal of pumpkin food. The adjoining fairytale garden makes it one of the few events on this list that holds the attention of a second-grader and a twelve-year-old at the same time.

Insider tip: the grounds ticket covers the playgrounds and fairytale garden too, so budget three hours, not one. Weekday afternoons are dramatically quieter than weekends, and the pumpkin regatta weekends are the busiest of the run.

Worth the drive · 9–12 October

🍷 Fellbacher Herbst — Wine and Harvest Festival

Marktplatz, Fellbach · 9–12 Oct · free entry

Fellbach's harvest festival takes over the town centre every second weekend of October: local vintners pouring the region's wines, a harvest parade, fairground rides and a closing fireworks display. It is a genuine village-scale Swabian fest rather than a tourist production, and the parade and rides make the daytime hours firmly family territory.

Insider tip: 20 minutes from Stuttgart on the U1 to Fellbach, which matters more here than usual — this is a wine fest and driving home is the wrong plan. The Sunday parade is the best single hour of the weekend for children.
